A grounded aircraft costs far more than a planned repair. On this Boeing 787 fleet, nose landing gear actuators are failing more often than expected. In four months of data, six of ten failed.

AcuMain tells managers which aircraft will fail in the next thirty days, before it happens.

The dashboard opens with the fleet picture: how many aircraft are high, medium and low risk, and one clear alert. Six aircraft are high risk, and without action about four of them will fail within thirty days.

The fleet outlook shows how fast the risk is growing: from no high-risk aircraft in January to six in April.

Every aircraft is ranked by risk, with a likely range and a clear decision: schedule repair now, plan an inspection, or no action needed.

Select any aircraft to see why. For B787-008, slow operation and very high leakage drive an eighty-five percent risk, and its history shows that risk climbing month by month.

Behind it is a model built not to overfit. Tested on aircraft it had never seen, it caught six of six failures with one false alarm. It scores new data the moment it arrives.
